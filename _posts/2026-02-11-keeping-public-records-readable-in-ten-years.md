---
title: "Keeping public records readable in ten years"
categories: ["Government"]
summary: "File formats outlive the software that made them, and not always gracefully. A note on choosing formats you will still be able to open."
---

A record you cannot open is not a record. Municipal offices tend to discover
this the slow way, when a request arrives for something filed in a format whose
software stopped shipping years ago.

The safest formats are the boring ones: plain text, CSV, PDF/A for anything that
needs to preserve layout. They are widely supported, readable without a licence,
and documented well enough that they can be recovered even if every current tool
disappeared.

The formats that cause trouble are the ones tied to a single vendor's product,
especially where the vendor also hosts the data. Ask what an export looks like
before you sign, not after. If the answer is a proprietary archive that only
their software reads, you have bought a subscription rather than a record.

None of this requires a migration project. It mostly requires asking the
question at purchase time, when you still have leverage.
